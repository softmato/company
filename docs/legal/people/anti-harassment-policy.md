# Anti-Harassment Policy

> **Draft — not reviewed by a lawyer.** Fill every `[confirm: …]` before use.
> This policy is **inert until a real name sits in section 5.**

The **Sexual Harassment at Workplace (Prevention) Act, 2071 (2014)** requires
an employer to prevent harassment at work and to have a mechanism for hearing
complaints. Having this policy is a legal obligation, not a nicety.

**Applies to everyone**, from their first day: employees, interns on every
track including unpaid, contractors, and the founders. It applies in the
office, at any place we work from, on client sites, at work events, and in
every channel we use — chat, email, video calls, and code review.

---

## 1. What is not acceptable

**Sexual harassment**, as the Act defines it, including:

- Unwelcome physical contact, or any physical advance
- Asking for sexual favours, or suggesting an advantage or a penalty in
  connection with one
- Showing or sending sexual images, messages, or jokes
- Comments about someone's body, appearance, or sexual life
- Persistently asking someone out after they have said no
- Any conduct of a sexual nature that makes someone's work environment hostile

**Other harassment and bullying**, which this policy treats with the same
seriousness even though the Act is narrower:

- Abuse based on caste, ethnicity, religion, gender, disability, age, marital
  status, or where someone is from
- Threats, humiliation, or shouting at someone
- Deliberately isolating someone, taking their work away, or setting them up to
  fail
- Spreading rumours about a colleague

**"It was a joke" is not a defence.** What matters is the effect on the person
it landed on, not what the speaker intended. If you are told something is
unwelcome, that is the end of it.

**Power makes it worse, not lighter.** Conduct by a founder, a manager, or a
supervisor toward someone junior — an intern especially — is treated as more
serious, not less, because the person on the other end may not feel able to
object.

## 2. What to do if it happens to you

You can do any of these, in any order. **You never have to confront the person
first**, and you never have to go through your own supervisor.

1. **Tell them to stop**, if you feel safe doing that. Sometimes it ends there.
2. **Tell the person named in section 5.** In person, by email, or in writing.
3. **Go outside the company.** You keep every right under the Act, including
   complaining to the police or the relevant authority, whether or not you tell
   us first. Nothing in this policy takes that away or requires you to exhaust
   an internal process before using it.

Write down what happened while it is fresh — what, when, where, who saw it. You
do not need evidence to make a complaint, but notes help.

**If you witness it**, say something. Report it under section 3 even if the
person affected has not. You will not be penalised for reporting in good faith.

## 3. What we do about it

1. **We acknowledge within [confirm: 2 working days]** and tell you what will
   happen next.
2. **We ask what you want.** Where it is possible and safe, your wishes shape
   how it is handled. Where the conduct is serious enough that we must act
   regardless, we tell you that plainly rather than acting behind you.
3. **We investigate promptly and fairly** — normally concluded within
   **[confirm: 15 working days]**. We hear the person complained about; they
   are told what is alleged and given a real chance to answer.
4. **We protect you during it.** That may mean changing reporting lines,
   separating schedules, or remote work. **Any interim change falls on the
   person complained about wherever possible, not on you.** You will not be
   moved, sidelined, or given lesser work because you complained.
5. **We tell you the outcome**, and what action followed.

**Confidentiality.** We tell only the people who need to know to investigate
and to keep you safe. We will not announce it to the team.

## 4. Consequences

Depending on what is found: a warning, an apology, training, a change of
duties, suspension, or dismissal without notice for serious misconduct. Where
the conduct is a criminal offence we may report it, and we will support you if
you choose to.

**This applies to the founders too.** Where a complaint concerns a founder, it
goes to the other founder and, at your choice, to an external person named in
section 5 — so there is always a route that does not run through the person
complained about.

## 6. No retaliation

**Nobody is penalised for making a complaint in good faith, for supporting
someone else's, or for giving evidence.** Retaliation — dismissal, demotion,
exclusion, worse work, a colder atmosphere — is itself serious misconduct and
is dealt with under this policy.

This protects you even if the complaint is not upheld. What is not protected is
a complaint someone knowingly fabricates, which is a separate matter.

An intern's placement, stipend, certificate, or reference is **never** affected
by having made a complaint.

## 7. Prevention

- Everyone reads this policy on their first day and signs that they have.
- We repeat it **[confirm: annually]** and whenever someone joins.
- The founders review complaints and outcomes **[confirm: annually]** to see
  whether something in how we work is producing them.
- We keep a confidential record of every complaint and its outcome, held under
  the **Individual Privacy Act, 2075** and separate from ordinary personnel
  files.
